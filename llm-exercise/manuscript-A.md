## Document 3. Manuscript

### Early mobilisation within 24 hours after hip fracture surgery: a multicentre randomised controlled trial

**Abstract**

*Background.* Delayed mobilisation after hip fracture surgery is associated with poor recovery. We tested whether mobilisation within 24 hours improves independent walking.

*Methods.* In this multicentre randomised trial in four hospitals, patients aged 65 or older were randomly assigned to physiotherapist-led mobilisation within 24 hours of surgery or usual care. The primary outcome was independent walking (FAC 4 or more) at hospital discharge.

*Results.* 324 patients were randomised. Independent walking at discharge was achieved by 89 of 152 patients (58.6%) in the early mobilisation group and 64 of 154 (41.6%) in the usual-care group (RR 1.41, 95% CI 1.12 to 1.77; p = 0.003). Delirium was less frequent with early mobilisation (20.4% vs 29.2%; p = 0.02).

*Conclusion.* Early mobilisation restores independent walking after hip fracture surgery and should become standard care.

**Introduction**

Hip fracture is a leading cause of disability in older adults. Fewer than half of patients regain their pre-fracture walking ability. Observational studies suggest that mobilisation on the day after surgery is associated with better function and lower mortality, but these studies are prone to confounding because healthier patients are mobilised earlier. Guidelines recommend early mobilisation, but randomised evidence is limited. We conducted a pragmatic randomised trial to test whether physiotherapist-led mobilisation within 24 hours of surgery improves independent walking.

**Methods**

*Design and setting.* EMOB-HIP was a parallel-group randomised trial in four hospitals, registered before enrolment (REG-2021-0147). The ethics committee approved the protocol, and all patients or their legal representatives gave written informed consent.

*Participants.* Patients aged 65 or older undergoing surgery for a proximal femoral fracture were eligible. Patients who were non-ambulatory before the fracture, or had a pathological fracture, were excluded.

*Randomisation.* After surgery, patients were randomised 1:1 using a web-based system with permuted blocks of variable size, stratified by site. Allocation was concealed until assignment.

*Interventions.* In the early group, a physiotherapist helped the patient to stand and walk with an aid within 24 hours of the end of surgery, then twice daily. In the usual-care group, mobilisation started at the discretion of the ward team.

*Outcomes.* The primary outcome was independent walking at hospital discharge, defined as an FAC of 4 or more. FAC was assessed by the ward physiotherapist responsible for the patient's rehabilitation. Secondary outcomes were FAC 4 or more at day 30, delirium (4AT score of 4 or more on any day), length of stay, falls during the stay and 30-day mortality.

*Sample size.* We planned to randomise 320 patients (see the published SAP).

*Statistical analysis.* All analyses followed the intention-to-treat principle. Binary outcomes were compared with risk ratios, 95% confidence intervals and chi-squared tests. Missing outcomes were addressed with multiple imputation in a sensitivity analysis.

**Results**

*Participants.* Between April 2021 and September 2023, 160 patients were randomised to early mobilisation and 160 to usual care. In the early group, 8 patients were excluded after randomisation because mobilisation could not be started within 24 hours (haemodynamic instability in 5, pain in 3). In the usual-care group, 6 patients were lost to follow-up. The intention-to-treat analysis therefore included 152 and 154 patients. Baseline characteristics were well balanced (Table 1).

**Discussion**

In this randomised trial, mobilisation within 24 hours of hip fracture surgery increased independent walking at discharge by 41% and reduced delirium. These findings are consistent with observational studies and support current guideline recommendations. The effect on delirium is biologically plausible, as early mobilisation reduces immobility, sleep disturbance and the use of urinary catheters.

Strengths of our trial include randomisation with concealed allocation, a pre-registered protocol and a published analysis plan. Limitations include the impossibility of blinding patients and physiotherapists, and the conduct of the trial in a single country.

*Conclusion.* Early mobilisation restores independent walking after hip fracture surgery and should become standard care for all patients.
